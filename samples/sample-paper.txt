The return of the wolf

A  When the last wild wolf in the region was shot in 1847, the event was reported in the local newspaper as a public service. For most of the following century the absence of large predators was simply taken for granted, and the deer population grew without check.

B  Ecologists began to notice the consequences only in the 1960s. Saplings were being eaten before they could establish; riverbanks, stripped of young willow, began to collapse. The term "trophic cascade" was coined to describe the chain of effects that runs downwards from a missing predator.

C  Reintroduction, when it was finally proposed, met fierce resistance from farmers. A compensation scheme, funded jointly by the regional government and a conservation charity, eventually secured a narrow majority in the local assembly.

D  Fifteen years on, the results are mixed. Deer numbers have fallen by roughly a third and the willow has returned along several stretches of river. Livestock losses, however, have been higher than the models predicted, and the compensation fund has twice been increased.
